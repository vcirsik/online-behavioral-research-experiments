# Online Behavioral Research Experiments

**Experimental Research | JavaScript | HTML | CSS | jsPsych**

## Overview

This repository contains browser-based behavioral experiments developed as part of peer-reviewed research investigating speech perception, auditory processing, and narrative comprehension under challenging listening conditions.

The projects demonstrate the design and technical implementation of experimental research protocols, including custom programming for stimulus presentation, experimental condition assignment, participant workflows, and behavioral response collection.

The experiments were developed using JavaScript, HTML, CSS, and jsPsych, and were deployed online using Pavlovia. Each project is organized into a separate directory with its own README documenting the research objectives, experimental design, and technical implementation.

## Research Projects

### 1. Speech Perception During Sentence and Story Listening

**Three Online Behavioral Experiments**

A series of experiments investigating age-related differences in speech perception across different listening contexts, including isolated sentences and continuous spoken narratives presented with background noise.

The experiments incorporated randomized experimental conditions, auditory stimulus presentation, speech-recall tasks, comprehension assessments, and participant screening procedures.

**Associated Publication:**

Irsik et al. (2022). *Age-related deficits in dip-listening evident for isolated sentences but not for spoken stories.*

### 2. Narrative Engagement Under Acoustic Masking

**Online Behavioral Experiment**

An experiment investigating speech perception, comprehension, and narrative engagement during continuous story listening in the presence of background noise.

The experiment incorporated randomized speech-recall prompts, precisely timed audio presentation, behavioral response collection, and comprehension and engagement assessments.

This behavioral study complemented a separate EEG experiment investigating neural responses to continuous speech.

**Associated Publication:**

Irsik et al. (2022). *Neural Activity during Story Listening Is Synchronized across Individuals Despite Acoustic Masking.*

## Technical Implementation

The experiments demonstrate custom development of browser-based research protocols using the following technologies:

- **JavaScript:** Experimental logic, randomization, stimulus sequencing, timing control, and behavioral response collection
- **HTML:** Experimental structure and participant-facing interfaces
- **CSS:** Interface styling and presentation
- **jsPsych 6.1.0:** Framework for implementing behavioral experiments and managing experimental procedures
- **Pavlovia:** Online experiment deployment and data collection

Across the projects, the implementations include:

- Experimental condition assignment and randomization
- Condition-specific auditory stimulus selection
- Timed stimulus presentation and task transitions
- Custom speech-recall and comprehension tasks
- Participant response collection
- Hearing-related and demographic questionnaires
- Volume adjustment, headphone verification, and practice procedures

## Repository Organization

Each research project is organized into its own directory containing the experimental implementation and supporting files.

Individual project READMEs provide additional information about the research objectives, experimental procedures, technical implementation, and associated publications.

## Data, Stimuli, and Dependencies

This repository contains experimental implementation code for research documentation and demonstration purposes.
Participant data and original audio stimuli are not included due to research confidentiality but can be requested. 
The experiments were originally developed using jsPsych 6.1.0, and the required library files are not included in this repository. Additional information about jsPsych is available at https://www.jspsych.org/.
Consequently, the experiments cannot be run in their entirety without the original stimuli and required dependencies.
